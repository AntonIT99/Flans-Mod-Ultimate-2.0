# Historical soldier name pools

The 32 `*_20th.json` lists target people serving during approximately
1914–1945: principally birth cohorts from the late nineteenth century through
the early 1920s, with older officers and late-war recruits also represented.
The menu label is **(20th century)** because the setting is the twentieth
century; it is not a birth-date restriction. Do not add postwar baby-name
favorites simply because they occur in a modern national list.

These are hand-curated pools of real name components. The references below
provide period context, spelling examples and starting points for further
verification. They are not bulk-imported census extracts, and the presence or
relative frequency of every entry has not been established for each army.
Common given names and surnames carry per-name weights (typically 20, 8 and 3 for
frequent, usual and less usual entries; unlisted entries weigh 1). These tiers
are hand-assigned from general knowledge of each period and language, not
measured census frequencies. The Belarusian, Tatar, Caucasian and Central
Asian groups of the Soviet pool and the Chinese and Japanese given names stay unweighted.
Group weights are illustrative game
mixes, not census or enlistment proportions. Names shared across periods are
intentional; historical given-name selection differs from the modern pools.
Surnames can be reused across periods without copying modern given-name pools.

No existing modern list or numeric assignment changes. Historical lists append
at slots 14–45 in the order below. The stable ID is `wolffsmodnpcs:<file stem>`.

## Naming conventions and scope

- British combines English/general British, Scottish and Welsh pools (8:1:1).
- Canadian combines English and French Canadian pools (3:1).
- Belgian combines Flemish/Dutch and Francophone pools (3:2).
- Jewish combines Ashkenazi and Sephardi pools (3:1), rather than using a modern
  Israeli-only pool. It is a naming tradition selector usable across armies.
- Russian contains Russian names only. Soviet includes exactly the same Russian
  pool plus Ukrainian, Belarusian, Tatar, Georgian, Armenian, Azerbaijani,
  Kazakh, Uzbek, Estonian, Latvian, Lithuanian, Romanian-speaking Moldovan,
  Kyrgyz, Tajik and Turkmen pools. Its emphasis is WWII; it does not imply that
  the Soviet Union existed during WWI. Russian/Ukrainian/Belarusian weights are
  50:20:6, Tatar 3, Caucasian/Kazakh/Uzbek 2 each, other groups 1 each.
- Hungarian, Chinese and Japanese place the surname first. Chinese uses
  tone-free Pinyin for consistent readable transliteration of period names,
  rather than mixing Pinyin and contemporary Wade–Giles spellings. Japanese
  uses romanization with long-vowel marks where supplied.
- Russian, Polish, Czech, Slovak, Latvian, Lithuanian, Bulgarian, Greek and
  relevant Soviet groups provide explicit female surname pools. Lithuanian
  female entries use unmarried forms; marital status is not simulated. Polish
  uses standard feminine adjective forms but does not generate historical
  marital-status suffixes on otherwise invariant surnames.
- Non-Latin-language pools use Latin transliteration. This is a presentation
  choice, not a claim about original script or a unique transliteration standard.
- Generated names are short display names. Patronymics, Iberian second surnames,
  titles, ranks and marital-name changes are not modeled. Whole compound given
  names and surnames are retained when included as one entry.
- Female pools cover period-appropriate names for women as well as male pools
  for men. Availability of a gender in the editor is not a claim about the
  historical composition of every country's frontline units.
- Spanish, Portuguese and Swedish pools use names from the same era even where
  the country's military experience differs from the principal belligerents.
  The selector does not assign uniforms, nationality metadata or allegiance.
